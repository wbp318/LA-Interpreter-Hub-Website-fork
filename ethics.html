Ethics & standards

Grounded in good practice.

A plain-language study aid for the principles most interpreter codes share.

Core principles

What most codes have in common

Confidentiality

Information from an assignment stays in the assignment. Share nothing identifying, even when asking peers for advice.

Accuracy

Convey the full message faithfully without adding, omitting or editorializing. If you need to clarify, say so openly.

Impartiality

Stay neutral. Disclose any conflict of interest and be ready to step aside.

Role boundaries

You interpret; you do not advise, advocate, or act as a witness or friend. Redirect politely when asked to step outside your role.

Study aid only. Your governing code of ethics, such as those published by NCIHC, RID or NAJIT depending on your field, is the authority. Always follow it.

Decision chart

When something unexpected comes up

A prompt for reflection, not a rulebook.

Text version of this chart
  1. Is anyone at immediate risk? If yes, follow the emergency protocol and alert staff.
  2. Is accuracy or understanding affected? If yes, pause, speak as the interpreter, and clarify.
  3. Is the request outside your role? If yes, politely state your role and redirect.
  4. Is there a conflict of interest? If yes, disclose it and offer to withdraw.
  5. Otherwise, continue and note it for your debrief.

Where the interpreter sits

Text version of this chart

  • The ethics code and confidentiality guide everything the interpreter does.
